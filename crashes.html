From 1792 to 2022: the boom before, the trigger, how deep, how long, who won. Tap a card to flip it. Index numbers come from Shiller's monthly S&P data (1871+) and published daily closes. Pre-1871 panics have no index, so those cards don't invent one.

Red = US stocks (daily-close Dow/S&P), orange = BTC. Rhyme: bigger drawdowns take far longer to recover, unless policy floods in (1987, 2020). BTC falls 77–93% but has recovered in 1.7–3.1 years every time so far.

Boom size vs crash size

X = % gain from the 5-year low to the peak (stocks, Shiller monthly) or the 2-year low (BTC). Log scale. Y = peak-to-trough drop. Rhyme: the biggest booms (1929, 1937, 1987, 2000) produced the biggest crashes, but 1987 shows a huge boom can get a short crash when the Fed steps in.

Patterns that repeat
  • Every crash follows credit-fueled growth in a "new era" asset: land, railroads, radio, internet, housing, crypto.
  • The trigger is almost always tighter money (1819, 1929, 1937, 2000, 2022) or a leveraged player failing (Duer, Cooke, Knickerbocker, Lehman, FTX).
  • Recovery speed depends on the policy response: no lender (1837, 1873, 1929–32) means years; a fast "unlimited" backstop (1987, 2020) means months.
  • What worked: cash/T-bills and no leverage going in, then buying when the policy pivot hits. Gold won in inflation crashes (1973, 1929→1934 revaluation). Shorts won only for those positioned before the trigger.
